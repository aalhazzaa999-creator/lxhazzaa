Search and filters

  1. Search by record ID, sample ID or archaeological site.
  2. Choose paternal Y-DNA or maternal mtDNA, then enter a branch. Descendants are included when the reference resolves their relationship.
  3. Filter by country, genetic sex, location type and spatial classification. Clear filters to start again.

No match does not prove a sample is absent from research; its record or assignment may be unavailable in this snapshot.

Map and location accuracy

A point is not necessarily a grave coordinate. Blue denotes an archaeological site, gold an approximate city, and clay red an approximate country point. Read location accuracy and its source.

Move the map, select a radius and update the area. The coordinates-only option includes approximate points. The map may limit displayed points for performance; record search still covers the available data.

Reading a remains record

Open a sample from results or a single map point. Review analysis ID, individual ID, dating, site and study. One person may have several analysis records; record totals are not automatically person totals.

Y and mt are independent fields. A male may have both. Missing Y for a female or any record is not a haplogroup assignment. Sex is taken from the source.

The tree link opens the available placement. Unresolved terminal calls stop at a supported ancestor rather than inventing a final branch.

Dating, quality and totals

BP means years before 1950. The mean-BP filter uses the reported mean, not overlap with the entire dating interval. Source quality labels such as Pass or Questionable do not independently verify a haplogroup.

Site totals and samples-per-site follow the current search. A named archaeological site is different from a map-point cluster.

Sources and interpretation

Academic sources include AADR on Harvard Dataverse. Consult the original study and dataset version attached to each record.

A haplogroup alone does not establish a modern tribe, ethnicity or personal migration. Unresolved spatial classifications remain unresolved without published evidence.
